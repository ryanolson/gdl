# Hot paths

This register lists the code paths that a profile shows to be hot, and the check that guards each one. A change to a path listed here runs the guard before and after, on the same host and build. A result outside the noise band needs its numbers and a reason in the change, and the owner's decision. Each entry point carries a `// hot path: <name>` comment, so a search finds the entry from the code. Each guard names its host. A number marked Neoverse-V2 history was taken on a quiet 72-core aarch64 Neoverse-V2 host and compares only with other Neoverse-V2 numbers. The current host is an 8-core x86_64 EPYC virtual machine ([Baselines on the x86 VM](benchmarking.md#baselines-on-the-x86-vm)).

The profiled workload is one stream of 100,000 items of 16 B through the lean two-segment graph, both ends in one process, on the Neoverse-V2 host (history), with gdl at main `0ff3a6b`, after the in-place encode. [Where an item's cycles go](crossing-performance.md#where-an-items-cycles-go) has the setup, the command, and the step split. Shares are of all sampled cycles: every thread, user and kernel. The local item boundary comes from an endpoint's profile, which its entry names.

The Neoverse-V2 guard numbers below are still under Cargo's default profile (A); the x86 VM lines are under the bench profile (B). They were taken before the workspace set its profiles and have not been measured again under the bench profile (B), which `cargo bench` now uses ([Release profiles](benchmarking.md#release-profiles)). So a guard run today does not compare with them: run base and change, both under the same profile, and compare those two.

## crossing-item-send

- **Entry point:** `crates/gdl-runtime/src/dist/hop.rs`, `pump_watching`. It takes each item, already encoded in place into a velo frame, from the producer's stream and hands it to `deliver`, which calls `Outbound::send` (`dist/fabric.rs`) and velo's `send_frame`.
- **Why it is hot:** it runs once for every item that leaves a segment.
- **Profile (Neoverse-V2 history):** `uv run --script scripts/bench/items.py profile --ref main=<commit> --out <new directory> --shape lean-16 --count 100000 --period 250000 --stack 8192 --cpus 16-23`, gdl `0ff3a6b`, 2026-10-26, a quiet 72-core aarch64 Neoverse-V2 host (load 2.8). 8.4% of 7,356 samples, callees included. `deliver` is 5.6% of it, and velo's `send_item_frame` 4.8%. At `d1fa15a` the entry held 7.0%.
- **Guard (Neoverse-V2 history):** `uv run --script scripts/bench/items.py count --ref base=<base commit> --ref change=<change commit> --out <new directory> --cpus 16-23 --rounds 7`: at `0ff3a6b`, under Cargo's default profile (A), 8,856 user instructions per item at 16 B, 10,336 at 1 KiB, and 28,942 at 16 KiB. Over seven rounds the range was -4% to +4% at 16 B and within 1.5% at the larger sizes. Repeated runs of one build gave medians within 1.2%. The script builds each ref under the bench profile its own manifest sets, so a base from before the workspace set its profiles builds under A; set `CARGO_PROFILE_BENCH_CODEGEN_UNITS=1 CARGO_PROFILE_BENCH_LTO=thin` to build both under B. Under B, at `51b8015`, the lean graph ran 7,806, 9,303, and 27,486 ([Release profiles](benchmarking.md#release-profiles)). `timeout 120 cargo nextest run -p gdl-runtime --test item_allocs --no-capture` fails when a crossed item makes more than half an allocation over velo direct.
- **Guard on the x86 VM:** the `lean-<size>` and `direct-<size>` shapes of the `requests` bench under callgrind, 1,000 and 2,000 items, base and change in one job ([Callgrind Ir](benchmarking.md#callgrind-ir)). `items.py` cannot run there, because it builds in a git worktree and the runner allows one checkout. At `54abbba`, seven rounds: 6,603 Ir per item at 16 B (range 6,568 to 6,703), 8,780 at 1 KiB, and 127,897 at 16 KiB.
- **Sensitive to:** a per-item poll of the stop, cancel, or deadline futures, a timer per item, a lock or an allocation per send.

## crossing-item-receive

- **Entry point:** `crates/gdl-runtime/src/dist/hop.rs`, `InboundItems::poll_next`. At the outer end it reads `SharedInbound::poll_next` (`dist/fabric.rs`), which locks a mutex on each poll and reads `Inbound::poll_next`. That takes each item from velo's `poll_next_bytes` as velo's view of the frame.
- **Why it is hot:** it runs once for every item that enters a segment. Its share is under 5%, and it is listed because it runs on every item and is the other end of the same path.
- **Profile (Neoverse-V2 history):** the same run as above. 3.9% of 7,356 samples, callees included. The `poll_next` functions of `dist/fabric.rs` are 3.9% of it, and velo's `poll_next_bytes` 3.4%. At `d1fa15a` the entry held 4.1%.
- **Guard (Neoverse-V2 history):** the same instruction count and allocation test as `crossing-item-send`, which count both ends. For the codec steps alone: `cargo bench -p gdl-runtime --bench remote -- 'codec-' --warm-up-time 1 --measurement-time 3`. In place, the whole encode and decode was 47.6 ns at 16 B, 105.9 ns at 1 KiB, and 456.6 ns at 16 KiB ([Codec cost](crossing-performance.md#codec-cost) has the setup). velo direct, the same function in each run, measured 66.8 to 69.1 ns at 16 B and 133.2 to 144.1 ns at 1 KiB, so the band is about 3% at 16 B and 8% at 1 KiB.
- **Unconfirmed on the x86 VM (wall time, not a guard until a host that can time is in place):** the codec rows, pinned to core 7, at `8b182e3`, three runs. The whole encode and decode in place took 60 to 61 ns at 16 B and 111 to 116 ns at 1 KiB. Velo direct took 80 to 83 ns and 157 to 167 ns. The 16 KiB rows are bimodal on this host and do not guard ([Wall time](benchmarking.md#wall-time)). The instruction count is the callgrind Ir row of `crossing-item-send`.
- **Sensitive to:** a copy of the payload out of velo's buffer, an owned `Vec` per item, a lock or an allocation per item.

## local-item-boundary

- **Entry point:** `crates/gdl/src/items.rs`, `Fuse::poll_next` (a node boundary) and `Bounded::poll_next` (an attempt boundary). Each item that leaves a local hop passes one attempt boundary, and one node boundary for its inner end and for each backward node but the last. In a hop with no backward node, the attempt boundary holds the inner end's node boundary unboxed, with `Fuse::poll_next` inlined into its own poll. The attempt boundary's endings (`finish`, `interrupt`) run out of line, and only the root's attempt (`bound_measured`) tests for its first item.
- **Why it is hot:** it runs once for every item at every local hop. An endpoint's streamed chunk crosses two hops.
- **Profile (Neoverse-V2 history):** dynamo-http-endpoints' `crates/http-endpoints/examples/loopback_profile.sh chat-stream` (`perf record -F 1999 -g`, frame pointers), with gdl at `523afc0`, 2026-10-27, a quiet 72-core aarch64 Neoverse-V2 host. On the 256-chunk streams of three APIs, `Fuse` held 12.7% to 13.8% and `Bounded` 1.8% to 3.1% of the server threads' samples, callees included. `__memcpy_sve` was the top leaf. With the copies cut in that gdl, `Bounded` held 0.8%, and `Fuse` 13.3%, most of it the endpoint's own stream below it ([Copies of an item](feature-costs.md#copies-of-an-item)).
- **Guard (Neoverse-V2 history):** `timeout 120 cargo nextest run -p gdl --test codegen` fails if either boundary's release code copies an item. `cargo bench -p gdl-runtime --bench hop -- 'chunks/' --warm-up-time 1 --measurement-time 3`: at `4931dcaf`, 2.07 to 2.10 µs for one request and 16.8 to 17.2 µs for 256 items, three runs on the Neoverse-V2 host within 2.6%.
- **Unconfirmed on the x86 VM (wall time, not a guard until a host that can time is in place):** the same `chunks/` command, pinned to cores 4 to 7, at `8b182e3`: 1,304 to 1,350 ns for one request and 24.7 to 28.5 us for 256 items, three runs ([Wall time](benchmarking.md#wall-time)).
- **Profile in callgrind Ir (a shared 20-core aarch64 host):** `crates/gdl/examples/stages.rs`, `operator u64 5 20000 1`, gdl `71b9fb92`, 2026-11-03, valgrind 3.28.0. Self Ir of the whole run: `Bounded::poll_next` 40%, `Fuse::poll_next` 20%, and the operators' own `map` 20%. With 512 B items, `memcpy` makes 3 calls per item per stage in the `plain` control as in `operator` and `hop`: the copies are the nodes' own `map`, and a boundary copies no item.
- **Guard in callgrind Ir (a shared 20-core aarch64 host):** the `stages` example's `operator`, `hop`, and `plain` shapes at 0, 1, and 5 stages, `u64` and `large` items, base and change built in one target directory ([Stages in callgrind Ir](feature-costs.md#stages-in-callgrind-ir) has the commands). At `71b9fb92`, per stage: 120 Ir per item for `operator`, 56 for `hop`, and 33 for `plain` with `u64` items; 356, 286, and 261 with 512 B items; 5,120, 1,956, and 252 Ir per request. Three rounds agreed within 2 Ir, so a change of more than 2 Ir per item is outside the band. Since one scope node holds a context's stop and cancel, an `operator` stage costs 2,814 Ir and 6 allocations per request, and a `hop` stage 987 Ir; the per-item rows moved by less than 1 Ir. Since a hop with no backward node holds its node boundary unboxed, an `operator` stage costs 90.3 Ir per item (320.2 at 512 B), and 2,642 Ir and 5 allocations per request.
- **Profile per instruction (the same host):** `operator u64 5`, gdl `993af293`, 2026-11-03, callgrind `--dump-instr=yes` at 20,000 minus 10,000 items. Of 559.4 Ir per item, the six attempt boundaries held 327.6 (54.6 each), the five operators' `map` 165.5, and the check every 64 items 5.0. Of one boundary's 54.6: 14 to save and restore registers, 6 to test that it has not ended, 16 for the two boundaries' yield tests and counts and the node boundary's test that it has not ended, 7 for the count to the next check, 5 for the dynamic call, 4 to test the poll, and 2 to test for the first item ([Where an attempt boundary's instructions go](feature-costs.md#stages-in-callgrind-ir)).
- **Guard at the three changes after it:** an `operator` stage costs 81.2 Ir per item (311.2 at 512 B), one hop 98.8 (372.9), and the stage 2,620 Ir per request (3,008 at 512 B). With a deadline (`stages ... deadline`): 84.6 Ir per item and 3,054 per request.
- **Sensitive to:** a call while the poll is held, even a call that returns a constant; an item moved into a new poll, or a poll built aside and copied into the return; a lock, a clock read, or an allocation per item; code inlined into the poll that runs once per stream, whose calls keep registers saved on every poll.

## roster-stream-retirement

- **Entry point:** `crates/gdl-runtime/src/dist/fabric.rs`, `Outbound::finish`. Each stream a segment sends waits there for velo's `retired()` before it gives back its admission share and egress permit.
- **Why it is guarded:** it runs once for every stream sent across, so it scales with the crossings of a request. It is not listed from a profile. It is listed because its cost is over the 1% per-request gate and the owner accepts it for now, on condition that it is watched.
- **Cost (Neoverse-V2 history):** on a quiet 72-core aarch64 Neoverse-V2 host, the commit before live rosters (`1ca7eba`) against the commit that added them (`83e9588`): three segments 546,726 → 558,658 user instructions per request (+2.18%), two segments +1.10%, one segment (the control) within the spread ([Live rosters](benchmarking.md#live-rosters)).
- **Cost on the x86 VM:** callgrind Ir per request, 200 and 400 requests, seven rounds, the three builds in one job ([Live rosters in callgrind Ir](benchmarking.md#live-rosters-in-callgrind-ir)). Two segments 277,027 → 278,964 (+0.70%), beyond the spread. Three segments 491,043 → 484,560, inside a spread of ±3%, so three segments cannot show a change of 2% here. At 400 and 800 requests three segments still spread ±1.4%, with +1.74% inside it. On this host the two-segment row is the guard. Main at `8b182e3`: two segments 274,344, three 478,836.
- **Guard:** the `requests` bench, base and change built in one target directory and run in turn. On a host where `perf` counts, setup `three` by user instructions, ten rounds, as [Claims checked on the Neoverse-V2 host](benchmarking.md#setup) shows. On the x86 VM, setup `two` by callgrind Ir, as [Callgrind Ir](benchmarking.md#callgrind-ir) shows, with `three` reported beside it. Compare only numbers from one host. A change to rosters or to stream retirement runs it and reports the guard row with its numbers. Growth beyond the range of the base goes to the owner before merge.
- **Sensitive to:** a lock, a waker, or an allocation per stream sent; a wake added to the pump's task; a second wait per stream.

## pool-pick

- **Entry point:** `crates/gdl-runtime/src/dist/pools/crossing.rs`, `Crossing::pool_by`. It calls the class's pool policy once per attempt (gdl's `RoundRobin::choose` and `HomeFirst::choose`, or a registered policy) and takes the choice apart. A choice with a lease is kept by `PoolChoice::with_lease` in one block (`crates/gdl/src/policy/thin.rs`).
- **Why it is guarded:** it runs once for every attempt of every hand-off into a class, and a set router's policy runs inside it. It is not listed from a profile. It is listed because its instruction count and its allocations are what policy changes are judged by ([Pool policies](feature-costs.md#pool-policies)).
- **Guard:** callgrind Ir per pick over `crates/gdl/examples/picks.rs` (`home_first`, `one_shard`, `round_robin`, `leased`), base and change built in turn in one checkout and one target directory, as [One allocation for a lease](feature-costs.md#one-allocation-for-a-lease) shows. On the shared 20-core aarch64 host, rounds repeat exactly, and code placement alone moves a pick by up to 10 Ir, so a change outside 10 Ir is a change. At the change that kept a lease in one block, under B: `home_first` 1,717.8, `round_robin` 643.0, `leased` 1,933.8. `timeout 120 cargo nextest run -p gdl --test leases` fails when a leased pick allocates more than once. In `gdl-runtime`, `a_choice_with_a_lease_allocates_once` fails when a leased pick through a crossing allocates more than once, and `a_pick_allocates_nothing_under_either_policy` when a pick without a lease allocates.
- **Sensitive to:** an allocation per pick or per lease, a fat pointer in `PoolChoice`, a pass over the pools that calls out of line for each pool, a lock or a clock read per pick.
